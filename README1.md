**Automatic Tunnel Lighting System Using LDR**

**📌 Project Description**

This project is a simple automatic tunnel lighting system designed to control a light based on the surrounding light intensity. An LDR (Light Dependent Resistor) is used to detect sunlight. During the daytime, sufficient sunlight is available inside the tunnel, so the LED remains OFF. In the evening, when the sunlight decreases, the LDR detects the lower light intensity and the LED automatically turns ON.

**🎯 Purpose / Product Use**

1.To provide automatic lighting inside tunnels.

2.To reduce unnecessary electricity consumption during daytime.

3.To improve visibility when natural light becomes insufficient.

4.To demonstrate the practical use of an LDR-based automatic lighting system.

This concept can be used in road tunnels, corridors, pathways and other areas requiring automatic lighting.

**🛠️ Materials Used**

3.LED

**🔧 How We Made It**

1.First, we created a tunnel-shaped structure using cardboard and coloured paper.

2.We placed the LED inside the tunnel to represent the tunnel light.

3.The LDR was connected to the circuit on the breadboard.

4.The resistor and LED were connected along with the LDR and power supply.

5.The LDR was positioned so that it could sense the surrounding light intensity.

6.When there is enough sunlight, the circuit keeps the LED OFF.

7.When the sunlight becomes low, the circuit automatically turns the LED ON.

8.We tested the model under different light conditions to verify its operation.

**⚙️ Working Principle**

The system works based on the light-sensitive property of the LDR. The resistance of an LDR changes according to the intensity of light falling on it. The circuit uses this change to control the LED.

Bright light → LDR senses sunlight → LED OFF

Low light → LDR senses darkness → LED ON
